Projectile at 20 m/s and 30°: height, time and range

Split the launch speed into a sideways part and an upward part. Gravity only slows the upward part, so solve the vertical motion for time, then use that time for the horizontal distance.

Split the velocity

the step everything else depends on
Velocity componentsfig. 1
The blue arrow is the kick. Green is how fast it moves sideways, red is how fast it rises.

Use the angle to split v₀ into two independent motions:

vₓ = v₀ cos θ = 20 × cos 30° = 17.32 m/s
vᵧ = v₀ sin θ = 20 × sin 30° = 10.0 m/s

vₓ never changes (nothing pushes sideways). vᵧ drops by 9.8 m/s every second because of gravity.

cos goes with the side touching the angle ✓
3

Solve it, step by step

tap a step to see why
  1. 1

    Time to reach the top

    At the highest point the ball stops rising for an instant, so vᵧ = 0.

    0 = vᵧ − g·tup  →  tup = vᵧg = 109.8 = 1.02 s
    why vᵧ = 0 at the top?

    Before the top it's still going up (vᵧ > 0), after the top it's coming down (vᵧ < 0). The switch happens exactly where vᵧ is zero. The ball is still moving sideways at 17.32 m/s.

  2. 2

    Maximum height

    Use the vertical motion only, with the time we just found.

    H = vᵧ·tup − ½g·tup² = 10(1.02) − 4.9(1.02)² = 5.10 m
    shortcut: H = vᵧ²2g = 10019.6 = 5.10 m ✓
  3. 3

    Total time in the air

    Going up and coming down take the same time when it lands at launch height.

    T = 2 × tup = 2 × 1.02 = 2.04 s
    the most-missed step: don't stop at 1.02 s
  4. 4

    Range (how far it lands)

    Sideways speed is constant, so distance = speed × time.

    R = vₓ × T = 17.32 × 2.041 = 35.3 m
  5. 5

    Check with the range formula

    A second method that should give the same number.

    R = v₀² sin 2θg = 400 × sin 60°9.8 = 346.49.8 = 35.3 m ✓

Common mistakes

where marks are lost
!

Watch out for these

  • Using cos θ for the vertical part. Vertical uses sin θ.
  • Stopping at 1.02 s. That's only the way up; the total is 2.04 s.
  • Mixing signs for g. Pick "up is positive" and keep g = −9.8 in vertical equations.
  • Calculator in radians. sin 30 in radians is −0.99, not 0.5.
✓

Quick sanity check

  • At 60° the range is the same (35.3 m) but the ball goes 3× higher. Complementary angles share a range.
  • H should be much smaller than R for low angles. 5.1 m vs 35.3 m, fine.

Check yourself

2 quick questions

If the same ball is kicked at 60° instead, what is the range?

sin(2 × 60°) = sin 120° = sin 60°, so the range is the same: 35.3 m. It just goes higher.

At the very top of the path, what is the ball's speed?

Only vᵧ is zero at the top. It still moves sideways at vₓ = 17.32 m/s.
